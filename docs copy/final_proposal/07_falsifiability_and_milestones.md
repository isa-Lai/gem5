## 1. Pre-Committed Scientific Decision Rules (Section 12 Matrix)

A hallmark of a top-tier ISCA/MICRO/ASPLOS/HPCA paper is scientific falsifiability: the experimental campaign tests an explicit hypothesis rather than cherry-picking favourable points to force a predetermined narrative. We pre-commit to the following decision rules from Section 12 based on the outcome of the controlled $2\times 2$ comparison (Cells A, B, C, D) and resource baselines:

| Experimental Result Observed | Pre-Committed Architectural Decision & Paper Framing |
| :--- | :--- |
| **1. Cell B improves over current COMPASS (A), with ready-data admission blocking (`blocked_while_ready_at_iMC`) reduced** | **Prioritise Delivery Representation & Protocol Cost:** Center the paper on bounded interval delivery, slot/credit conservation, and the hardware cost savings of replacing per-line speculative MSHRs and request messages. |
| **2. Cell C improves `iMC` readiness and reduces exposed demand latency, while Cell B adds little** | **Prioritise Independent Preparation ($K$ segments) & Future-Page Support:** Center the paper on advancing controller-side preparation across row/page segments ahead of cache-side triggers, plus `srTLB`/translation reach. |
| **3. Cell D improves significantly beyond both B and C ($I_{\text{prep}\times\text{del}} > 0$), and exchanged progress/readiness/credit state explains the synergy** | **Develop the Coordinated Two-Stage Claim:** Present the full two-stage COMPASS v2 architecture, demonstrating why deeper controller preparation requires amortised interval delivery to avoid LLC MSHR choking, and vice versa. |
| **4. Gains over current COMPASS disappear after a small, affordable baseline resource increase (e.g., 32/64 MSHRs or the prefetch-MSHR pool)** | **Quantify Resource & Representation Efficiency — Do Not Claim an Inherent Throughput Advantage:** Frame the contribution as a **lower-cost / lower-CAM-power representation** that achieves the performance of a larger MSHR pool with compact non-associative bitmaps and fewer interconnect request messages. |
| **5. Diagnostic Oracles show substantial headroom, but bounded variants (B, C, D) do not capture it** | **Diagnose the Blocking Restriction Before Adding Complexity:** Use the telemetry counters (`prep_blocked_unmapped_page`, `slot_reservation_stall`, credit return latency, `actq` caps) to identify whether missing page mappings, credit starvation, feedback timing, scheduling, or buffer capacity blocks the bounded design. |
| **6. No meaningful headroom remains relative to current COMPASS on a workload class** (e.g., `mvt`, `bicg`, `gemver`) | **Retain the Measured Current-System Result & Stop Adding Mechanisms for That Class:** Report those workloads honestly as low-headroom controls where existing COMPASS already saturates available supply latency. |
| **7. Throughput gains depend on starving a bystander program (`chase`, `heat-3d`) or rely on uncounted simulator structures** | **Repair or Narrow the Claim Immediately:** Do not treat a fairness regression or uncounted storage as a valid speedup. Audit `readq` $\to$ `actq` $\to$ write-mode starvation (Gate 4) and disclose all state before claiming success. |

> **Scientific Integrity Principle:** Completing the prototype does not oblige the paper to claim it as a universal throughput win. An initial result may establish higher throughput on constrained/long paths, lower-cost performance equivalence to a large MSHR pool, or a negative outcome that bounds where interval protocols help. Separate demonstrated empirical conclusions from the initial research objective.

## 2. Architecture-First Execution Order (Section 13 Deliverable Sequence)

The immediate priority is a **working interval-delivery prototype** alongside the minimum supporting ablations—not deferring architectural prototyping until every legacy campaign cell or oracle sweep finishes. We execute the research in six strict, gate-checked orders:

| Order | Concrete Deliverable | Primary Architectural Question Answered | Prerequisite & Validation Gate |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Order 1** | **Protocol & State Specification:** Specify the fixed Interval Descriptor format, `iPF` slot mapping, credit-return event/message, completion packet identity (`Cid, Did, Epoch, Offset`), and ordinary-demand fallback / merge rules. | *What exact per-line request/MSHR work does this protocol replace, and what bounded hardware state replaces it?* | Complete bit-level table accounting (Doc `04`) with zero unbounded simulator maps. |
| **Order 2** | **Read-Only Interval Delivery Prototype (Cell B):** Implement interval delivery in gem5 using current `iMC` preparation, initially restricted to **private read-only mapped streams** (`ddot`, then a second clean read-only kernel) and **page-bounded intervals**. | *Can existing ready data at `iMC` be delivered to the `iPF Buffer` through a compact, credit-controlled representation without per-line speculative MSHRs?* | Pass **Gates 1 & 6**: retain exact `iPF` data-buffer capacity, charge all control/data messages on the interconnect (no zero-time side channels), and verify partial intervals, out-of-order returns, descriptor replacement, demand catch-up, and invalidation. |
| **Order 3** | **Iso-Capacity & Mechanism Attribution (Cell B vs. Baselines):** Compare actual interval delivery (Cell B) at `8c/2ch`, `120` cycles each way (plus `40`-cycle sensitivity), `16/32` MSHRs against: (1) Current COMPASS (`16/32` MSHRs), (2) Current COMPASS + separate 64-entry prefetch-MSHR pool (`plan_B_delivery.md`), (3) Current COMPASS + `64/128` ordinary MSHRs, and (4) Strengthened `iPF` baseline. | *Is the observed gain attributable to the interval protocol and representation, or simply to added request-tracking capacity?* | Verify whether `blocked_while_ready_at_iMC` and demand wait cycles drop under Cell B, and compare hardware tracking cost against the 64-entry prefetch-MSHR pool. |
| **Order 4** | **Extended Independent Preparation Prototype (Cell C):** Add the fixed future-segment preparation window ($K \in \{1, 2, 4\}$ mapped segments per stream) at the `iMC`, with explicit `srTLB` mapping availability and charged control/seed traffic. | *Does advancing preparation across $K$ future segments without waiting for individual LLC triggers supply useful work that the original trigger path could not expose?* | Verify that Cell B did not silently use $K$-segment lookahead, and measure `iMC` readiness hit rate and candidate queue losses. |
| **Order 5** | **Complete Controlled $2\times 2$ Matrix (Cells A, B, C, D):** Evaluate A, B, C, and D across selected informative operating points (`40, 80, 120` cycles; `16, 32, 64, 128` MSHRs), always including the **64-MSHR control**. | *Do independent preparation and interval delivery improve performance individually or interact synergistically ($I_{\text{prep}\times\text{del}}$), and where does the benefit saturate?* | Remeasure **frontier feedback** and **presence filtering** ablations (including joint removal) on the frozen default to anchor the baseline explanation. |
| **Order 6** | **Freeze Candidate, Validate Writes, & Run Transfer/Interference Campaign:** Validate write/invalidation/store-merge correctness first, then evaluate `daxpy` and `dscal`, development mixes (`4+4`), 16-core scaling (`8+8`, `15+1`), held-out mixes (`jacobi-2d`, `atax`, `seidel-2d`, `gemver`), and bystander interference (`chase`, `heat-3d`, column walks). | *Does the frozen COMPASS v2 mechanism transfer cleanly to RMW streams, multi-program mixes, larger core counts, and held-out stencils without starving bystanders?* | Pass **Gates 2, 3, 4, 5**: full functional output verification on RMW kernels, separated development vs. held-out reporting, and full latency-tail disclosure. |

## 3. Implementation Guardrails for Order 2 and Order 3

To prevent common simulator shortcuts from invalidating the architectural conclusions, the implementation of Orders 2–6 must enforce four strict guardrails:

1. **No Relabelling of the Prefetch-MSHR Pool:** The prototype **must** replace individual LLC-to-`iMC` initiating request packets with interval authorisation messages and replace speculative per-line MSHR allocations with the defined Interval Descriptor and receiver-slot bitmaps. Merely renaming the 64-entry prefetch-MSHR pool from `plan_B_delivery.md` as "credits" does not test the interval protocol.
2. **Charged Interconnect Transport Only:** Every interval authorisation, preparation seed, credit replenishment message, descriptor revocation, and data completion must traverse the simulated interconnect with realistic delay and contention (and receiver backpressure). Uncharged side-band C++ function calls or zero-cycle buffer insertions are strictly prohibited.
3. **Explicit Simulator Simplification Disclosure:** If any secondary mechanism uses a bounded approximation in the initial prototype (e.g., fixed-size credit batching or page-bounded intervals instead of cross-page intervals), document the exact simplification in the experiment log and paper methodology section.
4. **Stopping Rule for Implementation Branches:** Stop an implementation branch once its actual mechanism has been tested and its limiting resource identified:
   - A small gain from Cell B with current preparation does **not** automatically reject extended preparation (Cell C/D)—check whether pulls in Cell B arrived before `iMC` data were ready.
   - A generic benefit from the conventional 64-entry prefetch-MSHR pool does **not** automatically reject amortised interval tracking—compare their control message traffic, CAM vs. bitmap hardware cost, and behaviour in Cell D.
   - Conversely, a repaired ready-at-controller oracle (Mode 2) still uses the ordinary per-line delivery path and therefore cannot by itself reject an interval representation.

## 4. Provisional Contribution Statement for the Paper (Conditional on Experiments)

Subject to the empirical outcomes of Orders 1–6, the target abstract and introduction contribution statement for the ISCA / MICRO / ASPLOS / HPCA manuscript is:

> **Provisional Contribution Statement:**  
> *COMPASS v2 exploits compiler-directed stream structure to coordinate DRAM-efficient preparation at the memory controller with credit-controlled interval delivery to the last-level cache. By decoupling a bounded $K$-segment controller preparation horizon from a compact, bitmap-tracked interval delivery protocol, COMPASS v2 enables useful memory preparation and data transfers beyond the concurrency limits of conventional per-line LLC prefetch requests and MSHRs. Across a controlled $2\times 2$ evaluation against tuned cache prefetching, standalone controller batching, and current COMPASS—including dedicated prefetch-MSHR pools and scaled MSHR budgets—we quantify which memory-hierarchy restriction is removed at each latency and concurrency operating point, establish the incremental benefit over existing COMPASS, and fully disclose the storage, control-message, and coherence costs of amortised stream tracking.*
